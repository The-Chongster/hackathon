// 01 / The Rules

Six Rules. No Exceptions.

Read these once. They keep things fair, fast, and fun. Anything not on this list is fair game — surprise us.

01
// Solo or Team
Hack alone or rally any size of team — two of you, ten of you, whatever works. No upper limit, no minimum.
02
// Build Window
Build over the weekend on your own schedule. Pre-baked code is not allowed; ideas, sketches, and notes are fine to bring in.
03
// Original Work Only
Open-source libraries, public APIs, and AI assistance are all encouraged. Cite what you used. Don't ship someone else's product as your own.
04
// No Vibe Coding
Use AI as a tool, not a driver. You should be able to explain every line of your project — what it does, why it's there, and how you'd change it.
05
// Ship A Demo
A working prototype beats a deck. Live demo on Sunday in front of the judging panel — see Format for the rules of the demo room.
06
// Be Decent, Have Fun
Code of Conduct applies the entire weekend. Be kind to your teammates and the rest of the floor. Sleep is allowed. Snacks provided.

// 03 / How It Runs

Loose by Day. Strict at Demos.

Build whenever and however suits your team. The weekend is yours — there's only one moment that's locked in.

// Build Time
Your Schedule
Hack from your sofa, the office, a cabin in the woods — wherever you do your best work. No mandatory check-ins, no enforced sprint blocks. Sleep when you need to.
// Mentors
Ask in Slack
Senior engineers, designers and product folk are on call all weekend in #htf-help. Drop a question, a screenshot or a half-formed idea — someone will jump in.
// Submitting
Repo + One Slide By demo time
Drop your repo link and a single title slide in the submissions form pinned to #htf. Anything submitted after demos start won't be judged.
